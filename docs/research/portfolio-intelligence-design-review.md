# Build the ledger first, the predictor last

**Executive summary.** The proposed design is mostly sound engineering pointed at the wrong bottleneck. At ₹20k of capital, two things will decide your results: friction and time. Model accuracy will not. A fully churned five-stock portfolio costs about **7.3% of capital a year** in fees, and a 25%-turnover monthly rebalance costs about **2.5%**. Short-term gains tax adds roughly **2.5% a year** if all gains are realised short-term. Meanwhile a buy-and-hold NIFTY 500 ETF would owe **no tax at all** on a decade of gains at this size, because the gain stays under the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption ([Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges/); [taxgarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/income-tax-on-sale-of-listed-shares-stcg-ltcg-india-ay-2026-27)) [derived; worked figures in the numbers section]. The best published machine-learning result for stock-level monthly prediction explains only about **0.4% of return variance** ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu 2020](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2223/5758276)). That implies a realistic AUC of roughly **0.52–0.56**. Even a genuinely skilled 4–6 stock portfolio would need **16 to more than 100 years** of live record to prove its skill statistically.

The verdicts follow from that. **Keep and strengthen** the plumbing: the point-in-time data archive, tax lots, cost model, portfolio analyser, deterministic rules and immutable suggestion ledger. Keep the trend, momentum and quality features as continuous inputs. **Modify** the prediction target: "beat the cap-weighted NIFTY 500 TRI" has a base rate below 50% that drifts with market breadth. Also modify the calibration method, the champion/challenger role and the LLM post-mortem. **Drop** chart patterns, candlesticks, news as a model input, "similar past cases" in the main view, and free-text lessons memory. On regulation, a purely personal tool sits outside SEBI's adviser and analyst rules, and that holds as long as nothing is shared for any benefit and no performance is claimed publicly. Two practical traps remain: NSE's terms of use ban automated scraping, and API order placement now needs a static IP.

## How to read the evidence tags

Each claim below carries a source link. Where the source is weak, a tag says so. **[peer-reviewed]** means a journal article. **[working paper]** means a university or SSRN paper not yet refereed. **[preprint]** means arXiv or similar, often very recent and unreviewed. **[press]** means news coverage of a document the researchers could not read directly; all SEBI loss-study figures fall here. **[practitioner]** means blogs, broker explainers or vendor pages. **[derived]** means arithmetic built on cited inputs, with the assumptions stated, and not a published number. **[unverified]** means the researchers could not confirm the claim this session. Several primary PDFs (NSE factsheets, Lo-Mamaysky-Wang, the SEBI circular bodies) could not be parsed. Those points come from abstracts or snippets and are flagged.

## The target definition is the hidden flaw in components 3 and 6

The factor research and the ML research reached the same conclusion independently, so it is the single most important design change. "Probability this stock beats the NIFTY 500 TRI" sounds natural but behaves badly, for three reasons.

**First, the base rate is not 50%.** Single-stock returns are extremely right-skewed: a few huge winners pull the cap-weighted index above the typical stock. Bessembinder et al. cover 64,000 global stocks over 1990–2020. They find that only **45.9% of stocks beat the value-weighted market in a given month and 43.2% in a given year**, with emerging markets at **45.0% and 40.8%** ([Bessembinder, Chen, Choi & Wei, FAJ 2023](https://r.jordan.im/download/investing/bessembinder2023.pdf)) [peer-reviewed]. India is the most concentrated of the 43 markets studied. Its top 5% of firms created **92.1% of gross wealth**, and only **24.2% of Indian stocks beat the market over their lifetimes** ([same paper](https://r.jordan.im/download/investing/bessembinder2023.pdf)). Interpolating the emerging-market figures gives a plausible base rate of about **45–46% at 20 days and 43–45% at 60 days** [derived]. Those figures include microcaps. No India-specific figure for NIFTY 500 constituents at these horizons was found, so compute it from your own data.

**Second, the base rate moves with market breadth.** In a broad small/mid-cap rally, more than half of NIFTY 500 names can beat the cap-weighted index. In a narrow large-cap year like 2025, far fewer do: the S&P India SmallCap fell 7.9% while the LargeMidCap rose 8.9% ([SPIVA India YE2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-india-scorecard-year-end-2025.pdf)). A shift in the base rate leaves the ranking metric (AUC) roughly unchanged but damages the probabilities themselves (Brier score). That was shown in a medical-risk study ([MDPI Symmetry 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/18/11/1936)) and transfers by analogy; no finance-specific study was found.

**Third, market beta dominates the label.** Low-volatility and quality stocks have betas around 0.76–0.79 against NIFTY 50 ([NSE Low Vol 30 factsheet](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/Nifty100_LowVolatility30.pdf)). They mechanically "lose" in rallies and "win" in sell-offs, even when they carry a real risk-adjusted premium.

**The fix:** make the model's target purely cross-sectional, and handle the market separately. Two options work. One is "beats the cross-sectional median, or the equal-weighted NIFTY 500", which has a 50% base rate by construction. The other is a beta-adjusted excess return (stock return minus β × index return). Show the current empirical base rate for "beats TRI" beside the score, rather than baking a moving intercept into the model. This also matters for component 6. The power calculation below shows that two models handling the base-rate intercept differently is exactly what makes champion/challenger comparisons noisiest.

## Component 1: inputs are right in kind, wrong in acquisition and completeness

The input list is good. Its two weaknesses are point-in-time completeness and how the data is obtained. No free price API is point-in-time safe by default. The one free source that is safe is the **raw NSE daily bhavcopy, archived yourself**: unadjusted, dated per session, and covering every traded security ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/has-nse-changed-bhavcopy-location/169551)). But NSE's terms of use prohibit "any systematic or automated data collection activities" ([NSE Terms of Use](https://www.nseindia.com/nse-terms-of-use)), with no personal-use carve-out found. So the cleanest data source conflicts with the cleanest acquisition method. You have to choose between compliance, cost and automation.

**Data-source economics at ₹20k.** Kite Connect costs ₹500 a month with historical data included ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/historical-data-and-live-market-data-payment-plan)). That is about **₹6,000 a year, or roughly 30% of your capital** [derived]. DhanHQ's data API is free only if you made 25 trades in the previous 30 days ([Dhan support](https://dhan.co/support/platforms/dhanhq-api/is-the-dhanhq-data-api-subscription-free-if-i-execute-a-minimum-number-of-trades-every-month/)), which is a direct incentive to overtrade. Broker data is also adjusted to today and mostly limited to current instruments, so it does not fix survivorship bias. At this capital, the defensible choice is manual archive downloads plus manual order entry. Revisit paid APIs when capital is perhaps 50–100× larger.

**Where the training history comes from.** Forward archiving gives you a clean record from today, but nothing to train or backtest on. Downloading about 15 years of daily files by hand is impractical, and scripting it runs into the terms-of-use ban. The cheapest honest route is a one-off backfill: subscribe to Kite Connect for a single month (₹500, about 2.5% of capital once rather than 30% a year), pull adjusted daily history, then cancel [derived from [Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/historical-data-and-live-market-data-payment-plan)]. That history is adjusted to today and mostly covers current instruments. Any backtest built on it therefore inherits **survivorship bias**, and the model gate in the build order must be read as optimistic until historical bhavcopies or a delisted universe are obtained some compliant way. Whether broker APIs serve delisted instruments' history was not verified.

## Component 2: keep the slow trend and quality signals, drop the pattern zoo

### Engine A, trend/technical: keep continuous measures, drop binary rules

The technical-analysis literature tells a consistent decay story. Brock-Lakonishok-LeBaron's moving-average and breakout rules looked strong on the Dow Jones from 1897 to 1986 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html)). Sullivan-Timmermann-White then corrected for data snooping across about 8,000 rule variants ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:54:y:1999:i:5:p:1647-1691)). Bajgrowicz and Scaillet found that "since 1962, most strategies have been unprofitable even at zero transaction costs" ([CFA digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/02/technical-trading-revisited-false-discoveries-persistence-tests-and-transaction-costs-digest)). In India, Mitra found that moving-average returns "cannot be exploited fully due to real world transaction costs" ([Quantitative Finance 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v11y2011i2p287-297.html)). In contrast, trend measured as 1–12-month returns is robust across 58 futures markets ([Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250)).

| Sub-component | Verdict | Evidence | Strongest counter-argument | Modification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-horizon returns, MA-distance, relative strength, volatility-scaled trend | **KEEP** | TSMOM over 1–12 months ([MOP 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250)). Indian momentum exists but is weakening, per a study of NSE 500 over 2005–2016 ([Sharma et al. 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:globus:v:22:y:2021:i:1:p:255-270)) | Indian anomalies are weakening, so trend may be gone by the time you deploy | Feed continuous values (1/3/6/12-month returns skipping the latest month, 50/200-day MA distance, rank against NIFTY 500 and sector) and let the model learn thresholds |
| Binary crossover "signals" | **DROP** | Do not survive costs or snooping correction ([Mitra 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v11y2011i2p287-297.html); [Bajgrowicz & Scaillet](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/02/technical-trading-revisited-false-discoveries-persistence-tests-and-transaction-costs-digest)) | Humans find crossover flags easy to read | Display them in the UI if you like, but never use them as a rule or feature |
| 1-month return | **ADD as its own feature** | A 20-day target sits near short-term-reversal territory [derived from the 1–12-month holding periods in the Indian factor evidence, e.g. [IIMA library](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/)] | Reversal is a short-horizon, high-turnover effect that costs will eat |  Keep it separate from 12-1 momentum, since it may carry the opposite sign |

### Engine C, quant factors: seven names, about three independent signals

Indian factor evidence is strongest for momentum and quality. The IIMA library reports **10.65% annualised** for momentum (WML) from 1994 to 2025 ([IIMA library](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/)) [working paper/data]. Quality-minus-junk earns **0.92% a month** of four-factor alpha, and **0.69% a month long-only** ([IIMA WP 2022](https://www.iima.ac.in/publication/performance-quality-factor-indian-equity-market)) [working paper]. Two caveats cut those numbers down. The library's size filter is light: it removed only 26.8% of stocks in 2019–20, so much of the premium comes from names far outside NIFTY 500. And with microcaps controlled, **65% of 452 published anomalies fail** a t ≥ 1.96 hurdle ([Hou, Xue & Zhang 2020](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394)) [peer-reviewed]. A haircut of **at least 30–60%** on published premia is prudent [derived]. That range is anchored on McLean and Pontiff's post-publication decay ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:71:y:2016:i:1:p:5-32)), but their exact headline percentages were [unverified] this session. A competing view argues most factors do replicate ([Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen 2023](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/95651880/theis_ingerslev_jensen_et_al_is_there_a_replication_crisis_in_finance_publishersversion.pdf)). Their final headline replication rate was not verified. This is **contested**, but both sides favour a few broad, economically motivated themes over many narrow signals.

### Engine E, fundamentals: needed, but blocked by data

Quality and value need fundamentals, and leakage-free fundamentals need **as-reported, release-dated** data. None of the free tools provide that for India. The canonical source, CMIE Prowess, appears only at institutional prices: a 2021 college invoice shows **₹2,55,000 a year** ([Bhavan's invoice](https://bhavansvc.ac.in/NAAC/criteria/criteria%203/3.5.2/CMIE%20Software(2020-21).pdf)), with individual access usually through a university ([IIM Calcutta library](https://library.iimcal.ac.in/cmie-prowessiq/)). **Verdict: MODIFY.** For the stocks you hold or watch, keep a forward-built, filing-stamped fundamentals table, typed from results filings as they arrive. For model training, start with price-based features only. A non-peer-reviewed Indian study using only 10 price characteristics reached a **0.55% monthly out-of-sample R²** ([SSRN 7339440](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7339440)) [working paper, snippet only]. That is comparable to GKX. Add quality and value to the model only once a point-in-time panel exists. **Counter-argument:** quality is the best-evidenced Indian factor, and dropping it from the model throws away the strongest signal. That is true. One cheap proxy is membership in the NSE Quality 30 or Value 30 indices, reconstructed point-in-time. This is an untested idea of this review.

## Component 3: LightGBM is fine; the probability it emits needs honest framing

Model choice is not the problem. In GKX's 30-year out-of-sample test, boosted trees reached **0.34% monthly R²** against **0.40% for the best neural net**, and 0.52–0.70% for the largest 1,000 stocks ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu 2020](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2223/5758276)) [peer-reviewed]. Gains shrink further once microcaps, costs and real-time feature selection are accounted for ([Avramov, Cheng & Metzker 2023](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4449); [Li, Rossi, Yan & Zheng 2025](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/JFE_2025.pdf)). An R² of 0.4% corresponds to a forecast-outcome correlation of about 0.063, a directional hit rate of about 52%, and an **AUC around 0.52–0.54** [derived].

## Component 4: the portfolio analyser is the best-evidenced piece for a ₹20k account

The strongest behavioural evidence is on your side here. A 4–6 stock account belongs to the group that benefits from diversification feedback.

## Component 5: make HOLD the default and gate every action on cost and tax

The behavioural record says advice layers tend to cause over-action. In Barber and Odean's study of US households, the most active quintile earned **11.4% a year against the market's 17.9%** ([Barber & Odean 2000](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/Returns.html)) [peer-reviewed; the overconfidence interpretation is contested]. SEBI's study of FY23 equity intraday trading found **71% of individuals lost money, rising to 80% among those with more than 500 trades a year** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)) [press]. No equivalent SEBI study of delivery trading was found. Default effects are among the strongest levers known: automatic enrolment raised 401(k) participation by about **48 percentage points** ([Choi et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/W8651)). The label set as proposed has four action-implying labels against one inaction label.

## Component 6: the ledger is the asset; the learning loop must stop chasing noise

An immutable suggestion ledger is the most valuable long-run artefact the system produces. It is your only honest record of what the system said, when, and with what inputs. The loop built on top of it, however, assumes individual outcomes carry learnable signal. With R² around 0.5%, more than 99% of any single outcome's variance is noise, and a perfectly calibrated 60% call is wrong 40% of the time [derived from [GKX](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2223/5758276)]. The evidence on retraining windows favours **expanding windows with annual refits** over short rolling ones. Li et al. report 0.40% a month (t = 2.34) for expanding windows against 0.33% (t = 1.35) for rolling ([Real-Time ML WP](https://cfet.frankfurt-school.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Real_Time_ML.pdf)). Five-year rolling windows "seem to be too short" ([J. Asset Management 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41260-023-00318-z)).

**The 3-month shadow cannot measure what it is meant to.** A power calculation [derived] makes this concrete. Detecting a realistic Brier improvement needs roughly **34 independent resolution dates if the two models' differences cancel across stocks, and roughly 950 if they share sector or level tilts**. That is about **3–75 years with 20-day labels and 8–225 years with 60-day labels**. Three months gives about three 20-day or one 60-day independent cross-section.

## Revised design: a tax-aware decision journal with a modest ranking model

The overall recommendation reframes the product. At ₹20k, the system's job is to **protect capital from friction and from your own over-trading**, record every decision honestly, and build the data and evaluation machinery that becomes valuable as capital grows. Proving alpha is not a realistic job for it. The economics change sharply with scale. At ₹10 lakh, trading costs at 25% monthly turnover fall to about **0.70% a year**, while the tax-deferral gap becomes about **1.5 pp a year**. The fixed costs that dominate at ₹20k (DP charges, data subscriptions, static IP) become rounding errors [derived from [Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/charges/) and [taxgarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/income-tax-on-sale-of-listed-shares-stcg-ltcg-india-ay-2026-27) rates].

**Build order and go/no-go gates.** Build in this order, because each step's value does not depend on the next step succeeding.

1. Build R1 and R2, then R3. This alone gives an honest after-tax scoreboard against the ETF.
2. Build R4 and R5 as offline research only, on the one-off backfilled history described under component 1. Because that history is survivorship-biased, passing the gate is necessary but not sufficient. The **go/no-go gate** before any suggestion reaches the UI has three conditions. Out-of-sample Brier skill against the time-varying base rate must be above zero over a purged walk-forward test. The top-minus-bottom NIFTY 500 spread must survive the Zerodha cost schedule. AUC must sit below the leakage alarm. If the model fails, the system still works as a ledger, analyser and tax tool, and the honest answer is to hold the ETF.
3. Build R6 and R8 together. Run them in paper mode for a few months to catch pipeline bugs, not to "prove" the model.
4. Add the R7 template.
5. Add the LLM layer and R9 last, if at all.

## Conclusion

The review changes the question the system should answer. "Which of my stocks will beat the index next month?" is close to unanswerable at this scale: the honest signal is a few points of lift on a 45% base rate, and verification takes decades. "What is the cheapest, most tax-efficient action that respects what the evidence actually supports?" is answerable, and answering it well usually means doing nothing. The deterministic parts of the design (lots, costs, tax timing, concentration, an append-only ledger) carry real and measurable value from day one. The predictive parts only earn a place in front of you after passing a pre-registered, purged, cost-aware test.

There are two implications. First, the most valuable output of the learning loop is calibration plus a turnover-against-hold comparison, not lessons, because that is what protects you from both algorithm aversion and over-trading. Second, the system's economics improve dramatically with capital, while its statistical problems do not. Building it now is sensible if the goal is to have a trustworthy archive, ledger and evaluation harness ready by the time fixed costs become negligible. It is not sensible if the goal is to beat a NIFTY 500 ETF with ₹20k this year.
